CPRE 288 · EMBEDDED SYSTEMS

CyBot /
JONES

A companion robot designed to help a visually impaired user navigate to a destination using environmental sensing, mapping, and autonomous driving.

Project overview

CPRE 288 introduced embedded systems through weekly labs that built toward a final CyBot project. During the first two-thirds of the course, Chingyam Yeung and I developed and tested code for the robot’s peripherals. For the final project, we joined another group and collaborated with Kelsey Graham, Chase Phelps, and Joey Brekelman to combine the course work into JONES, a companion robot intended to help a blind user cross a street safely.

Sensing, mapping, and control

JONES uses infrared scanners and ping sensors to observe obstacles ahead. A servo sweeps the sensors across the environment to collect readings from multiple angles, including both low and tall obstacles. We passed that data to a Python script that generated a physical map of the surrounding area. In manual mode, the map gave the operator enough context to drive the robot without directly watching it. In autonomous mode, the robot used the same observed data to choose a route on its own.

Final demonstration

For the final demo, JONES navigated autonomously to a glass plate on the floor that represented a hazardous drop or hole. Its downward-facing scanner detected the surface change, and the robot played a short song to tell the user that it had reached the destination. Along the way, sound also served as a direct communication channel between the companion robot and its user.
